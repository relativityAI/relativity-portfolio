# Theme and fundamentals (focus areas 6–7)

## 6. Theme and peers

**Why:** Capital flows into whole groups. If a stock is strong *and* its peers are strong, the move is more likely to last.

**Check:**
1. List 5–10 peers in the same industry.
2. For each: within 20% of the 52-week high? Stage 2? Positive RS?
3. **Pass:** several (roughly 3+ or a clear majority) are strong. A group where most names are far below highs = Fail.
4. Prefer the **leader** in the group (the one that already ran). Leaders keep running. Do not buy a laggard just because it has not moved yet.

**Method note:** Scan for strong stocks first. Count which industries have the most names near highs. The top 3–5 groups show where money is going. Starting from the sector first usually means the stocks have already moved by the time the sector is clear.

**Theme stories:** A real theme has a simple, believable story (e.g. gold price up → gold-loan growth; AI demand → memory chip prices up; GST cut → auto demand). Check the story is real in the numbers, not only in headlines.

## 7. Catalyst and fundamental sanity check

**Goal:** Explain *why* the chart is working, and rule out junk. Keep it basic. Do not build a full valuation model.

### Look for a catalyst (at least one should be clear)

- New product or new brand
- Geographic expansion (new cities/countries)
- Industry growth or demand boom (policy, price change, new cycle)
- Winning more business from existing clients (client mining)
- Market-share gain or more distribution
- Acquisition
- Capex that is now complete and in use (utilisation rising)
- Margin expansion: premium products, scale, backward integration
- **Operating leverage:** fixed costs already built, sales jump → margins jump
- Change in ownership or management (e.g. a strong new parent)
- Debt reduction, restructuring, or lower interest cost; promoter pledge removed
- Regulatory change or approvals
- Large new orders
- Demand-supply imbalance in the industry

### EPS momentum

- Are earnings (EPS) and estimates rising for several quarters? Rising estimates after a long sideways period often start a 1.5–2 year run.
- Check the last 8 quarters: sales growth, EBITDA margin, EPS. Is the trend up and getting better, flat, or slowing?
- Check recent management commentary (earnings call, investor presentation): are they guiding up or down?

### Red flags (any one can make the verdict "Avoid")

- Fraud, accounting issues, auditor resignation, CEO/CFO sudden exit
- Reported profit rising but **operating cash flow negative for years**
- Big order or "category" claims that cannot be verified
- High promoter pledge, rising debt, very high interest cost
- Order-book business whose order book is running out

### Growth slowdown and valuation

- **The first weak quarter after fast growth is the key danger**, especially at a high P/E. Growth falling from 30–40% to 10–15% often marks the start of a Stage 3 top.
- **Base effect:** For lumpy, order-based businesses, a huge year makes the next year's growth look like a collapse. Check whether the current profit is one-off.
- **Valuation sanity check:** Compare the current P/E with the stock's own history and with peers. If P/E is extreme *and* growth is slowing *and* the chart shows a vertical climax → strong Avoid. A good business at a bad price is a bad stock.
- A stock can still be in an uptrend while looking expensive. Use the trailing stop rather than guessing the top.

### Cross-check with the chart

- Chart strong + fundamentals strong → highest conviction.
- Chart strong + weak or unclear fundamentals → investigate; be cautious. Charts can run on hype; fundamentals protect you from junk.
- Chart weak + fundamentals strong → wait. Good companies can spend years in sideways "time correction". Do not buy until Stage 2 starts.

## Things to read for the latest context

- Quarterly results and the investor presentation
- Earnings call (look for guidance on growth, margins, capex, orders)
- Stock-exchange announcements: orders, buybacks, ownership changes, capacity additions
