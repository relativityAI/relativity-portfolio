# Chart checks (focus areas 1–5)

Use weekly charts for trend and stage. Use daily charts only to time entries.

## 1. Near 52-week high

**Why:** Demand pushes prices up. A stock with real demand sits near its high. A stock that "looks cheap" near its low rarely becomes a multibagger.

**Check:**
- Distance = (52W high − price) / 52W high.
- **Pass: within 20%.** Within 10% is stronger.
- Liquidity filters (Indian-market defaults, adjust to your market): market cap > ₹500 cr, price > ₹20, average traded value ≥ ₹5 cr/day over 10–12 days.

**Also note:** Strong stocks often hover near the high for 6–12+ weeks before the next leg. That gives time to study the business. Stocks on this list usually stay for a year or more.

## 2. Relative strength (RS)

**Meaning:** The stock's return compared with a benchmark index. Like your marks vs. the class topper, not vs. your own past marks.

**Check:**
1. Calculate stock return and index return for 1 month and 3 months (also 6 months for context).
2. **Pass:** the stock return is higher than the index in both. In a falling market, Pass if the stock fell less.
3. Visual check: stock line vs. index line (ratio chart). Rising ratio = Pass.

**Why 1–3 months:** One week to one month gives many false signals (news spikes). 6–12 months reacts too slowly.

**Do not confuse RS with RSI.** RSI compares a stock to its own last 14 periods. Strong stocks sit "overbought" for long stretches while still rising, so RSI is noise. Ignore RSI.

## 3. Stage analysis + 40-week MA

Use the **40-week EMA/MA** on the weekly chart (30-week is the classic Weinstein version; use one and stay consistent).

| Stage | What it looks like | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Long sideways base after a decline. MA flat. | Watch. Not yet a buy. |
| 2 | Price above a **rising** 40W MA. Higher highs, higher lows. | **Buy / hold.** |
| 3 | Price flat or choppy near the top. Cannot make a new high. MA flattening. | Reduce / do not buy. |
| 4 | Lower highs, lower lows. Price below a **falling** 40W MA. | Avoid / exit. |

**Check:** (a) price above or below 40W MA, (b) MA slope up/flat/down over the last 10 weeks, (c) last 3 swing highs and lows.

**Pullbacks are normal in Stage 2.** A 20–30% dip that holds above the 40W MA is not an exit. Several stocks fell 20–25% without breaching the 40W MA and then rose several-fold.

**Breakdown rule:** A first close below the 40W MA is a warning. Treat it as a real breakdown only when the stock *follows through* lower (see section 4). Example: a stock broke its 40W MA once, bounced back, and only broke down for real months later.

## 4. Base and breakout

**Breakout needs two things:**
1. A strong close above the resistance level.
2. **Follow-through** (the stock keeps going or holds above the level in the days/weeks after).
No follow-through = not a successful breakout. Think of punching a glass wall: a crack is not a break. The same logic applies to breakdowns.

**Long base (1+ year, often 2–6 years):**
- Usually a "sleeping" business. A breakout from a long base suggests something fundamental is changing.
- Long-base breakouts often produce the biggest multi-year moves (3x–10x+). Institutions enter, free float is limited, and strong hands collect shares.
- Look for a catalyst (see theme-and-fundamentals.md).

**Short base (3–12 weeks, after a move):**
- The stock moves sideways after a rise. This usually means *no new supply*, not weakness. The market is digesting the move and waiting for the next quarterly result / EPS momentum.
- **If the long-base breakout was missed, the first short-base breakout is a good entry.** Do not think "it already ran."

**Darvas box (for trending stocks):** Draw a box from each higher high to the higher low after it. In a healthy Stage 2 uptrend, boxes stack upward. A break below the latest box low is a warning.

**Checklist:**
- Where is the base (start, end, length)?
- What is the resistance level? Did price close clearly above it?
- Did it hold above after the breakout (follow-through)?
- Was the base tight (small weekly ranges) in the last few weeks?

## 5. Volume

**Check:**
- Is there a **volume spike** on the breakout or on an up-week? Compare with the 50-day average volume (a clear multiple, e.g. 2x+, is meaningful).
- A "sleeping" stock that suddenly rises on heavy volume deserves attention. It usually means institutions are buying.
- During pullbacks, volume should shrink. Heavy volume on down days is a warning.
- Price near a 52-week high *with* rising volume = confirmation.

## Top and exit signals

**Two kinds of tops:**

| Type | Signs | Cause | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Climax top** | Near-vertical rise, highest volume ever, extreme valuation (high P/E expansion), no base on the way up | Everyone already bought; any shock reverses it | Sharp, often one-bar reversal. Exit fast. |
| **Slow distribution** | Lower highs form over months; growth slowing; order book finishing | Internal story weakens | Gives time. Stock bounces several times before breaking down. |

**Stops:**
- Trail a stop on the weekly chart (under the last higher low or the 40W MA).
- Decide exit before entry. After that, do not interfere with the plan unless the thesis changes.
- Investors who use weekly charts avoid being shaken out by daily and intraday noise.

## Quick scoring guide

- Stage 2 + near high + RS positive + breakout on volume = strongest setup.
- Stage 2 + tight base under resistance = watchlist with a clear trigger.
- Stage 3/4, vertical climax, or a failed breakout (no follow-through) = avoid.
